## About This Project

This public history research project traces the story of **Caserne 10**, one of Montreal's oldest continuously operating fire stations, beginning with a small curiosity: a back lane near Sainte-Catherine and Guy streets called *"Rue de la Police."*

That street name — a fossil of a building demolished in 1931 — led to the discovery of two fire station buildings, seventy years of neighbourhood transformation documented in fire insurance atlases, and a catastrophe that killed over sixty infants one block away on Valentine's Day, 1918.

## The Grey Nuns Fire — February 14, 1918

On the evening of Valentine's Day, 1918, a fire broke out in the crèche dormitory of the Grey Nuns Motherhouse on Guy Street — one block from Caserne 10. Over 170 infants were asleep on the top floor. Sub-Chief Marin and firefighters from the Montreal Fire Brigade responded within minutes. Despite rescue efforts by firefighters, soldiers, and nuns, between 53 and 60+ infants perished.

The fire was initially attributed to an electrical short circuit. It was later determined to be **arson**, set by Berthe Courtemanche, a nursery worker hired six weeks before. She was found unfit to stand trial.

The `articles/` folder contains translations of eight French-language and one English-language newspaper accounts from February 1918, sourced from BAnQ Numérique.
