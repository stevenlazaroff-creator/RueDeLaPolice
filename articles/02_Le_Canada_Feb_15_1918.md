# Tragic Fire at the Grey Nuns' Convent on Guy Street

**Source:** *Le Canada*, Montreal, Friday, February 15, 1918, Vol. XV, No. 296, Page 8

---

## The Fire Breaks Out on the Floor Where the Crèche Was Located, Causing a Hecatomb of Babies

### Acts of Heroism

**The military hospital was evacuated without incident, along with the hospitalized. — Material losses are considerable.**

Fire at the Grey Nuns! Like a trail of gunpowder, this sinister rumor spread throughout the city last night, around eight o'clock.

Unfortunately, it was true, and a few moments later, the glow of the fire illuminated the horizon.

Everyone feared a catastrophe when they considered the importance of the institution and the considerable number of nuns, hospitalized persons sheltered under the roof of this establishment. It is providential that there were no more losses of life than those unfortunately suffered at the Crèche.

The terrible fire caused a hecatomb of babies — "the big babies," as the nuns call them. How many are there? Last night, it was impossible to give the exact number; but it is believed that the figure will rise to twenty-four or twenty-five, perhaps more, alas!

However, everything was done to save the lives of these little beings who only wanted to live. There were sublime acts of devotion among the nuns who courageously threw themselves into the dormitory where burning flames rolled and where asphyxiating smoke made the bravest retreat. Sister Côté deserves, however, a very special mention. A true heroine, she was returning for the fourth time to fetch babies and was emerging from the furnace with four children in her arms when a firefighter met her and had to order her not to return — the danger was too great.

From all sides, the Reverend Sisters of the mother house on Guy Street received the needed help. All the houses in the neighborhood opened their doors, and many sick people and babies were transported there and received the required care. At Mont Sainte-Marie, a large number were hospitalized.

Despite everything, the most admirable order never ceased to prevail, everyone offering the most admirable cooperation in the trial that the Grey Nuns were undergoing.

The soldiers hospitalized in the military hospital who could help with the rescue rendered great services. They bravely evacuated their severely wounded comrades, methodically, obeying the commands of their officers. Several of the sick had to be transported to hospitals.

His Grace Monseigneur Bruchési, as soon as He learned the sinister news, went to the mother house, and it was a great consolation for the nuns to see the Archbishop of Montreal share in their grief.

The Mayor of Montreal and other prominent figures rushed to express their sympathies to the nuns; they greatly appreciate the offers that were made to them in their misfortune and extend their most cordial thanks.

At 7:50, the private alarm No. 419 of the Grey Nuns' institution sounded the alarm; almost immediately, a second alarm called the second section of the brigade, then at 8:07, a third alarm called the reserve.

Upon their arrival at the scene of the fire, which was raging on the fifth floor — probably caused by a short circuit — of the wing facing Saint-Mathieu Street, the firefighters attended to the rescue of the soldiers and the children of the Crèche.

It was on this floor where more than two hundred children were sleeping in their cradles that the fire originated. The arrival of all the reinforcements enabled the firefighters to attack the fire, but the water pressure seemed weak; it was necessary to set up the steam pumps and lay hoses at a considerable distance.

The fire was making rapid progress; the entire floor and the roof were ablaze, threatening the central part of the building and the chapel. However, after a fierce struggle, the fire was contained in the body of the building where it had started, but everything is in ruins — what the fire did not reach has been ravaged by water and smoke.

From the section belonging to the military hospital, the accounting records and clothing reserves were saved; some pieces of furniture were also removed in time.

During the fire, two firefighters were injured by falling materials; they were treated on site by Dr. Lafleur, physician of the brigade; another was treated by Dr. Lamoureux, also a physician of the brigade.

The ambulances had a great deal of work to do transporting the injured to their institutions. To the General Hospital, they had to transport those whose names follow, most of whom are suffering from nervous shock: Sergeant Weir, Soldiers Alexander, O'Hara, Doherty, Olansby, Houle, and Mrs. Beaulieu, a nurse.

To the Royal Victoria: Soldier Jean Lessert, nervous shock.

To the Western Hospital: Sam Robert, soldier returned from the front.

A very large number of soldiers were received at the Western, but as soon as they were able to recover from the alarm, they were sent to the Khaki Club. Twelve wagons transported them.

At Notre-Dame Hospital, 25 patients were received in this institution; all are also suffering from nervous shock.

A very strong police service ensured order; it was commanded by Inspector Bourgeois; Chief Campeau and Sub-Chief Grandchamp went to the scene of the disaster.

A crowd of several thousand people filled Dorchester, Saint-Mathieu, and Guy streets.

### Notice to Soldiers

During the evening, Major Hall, commandant of the convalescent soldiers' hospital at the Grey Nuns, gave us the following communication: "All soldiers who were at the convalescent soldiers' hospital at the Grey Nuns are asked to report immediately to the central admission and discharge depot, 68 Drummond Street."
