# Twenty-Four of the Victims Identified — The Coroner's Inquest Begun This Morning, Adjourned to Next Monday

**Source:** *La Patrie*, Montreal, Monday, February 18, 1918, 39th Year, No. 298

---

## The Fire at the Grey Nuns' Convent

### Four Nuns Heard This Morning

The inquest into the death of the 58 children in the fire last Thursday evening at the Grey Nuns' convent was begun this morning before Coroner McMahon, without a jury. After questioning Sub-Chief of firefighters Marin and several nuns, the coroner adjourned the hearing of testimony to next Monday, when he will hear, among other witnesses, the institution's electrician, Mr. Doré.

The testimony heard this morning does not reveal the cause of this terrible fire. No evidence suggests that the fire was set by a criminal hand, and nothing — as Sub-Chief Marin declared — helps to trace the accidental cause.

When questioned, the nuns affirmed that there was nothing in the children's dormitory, in the recreation room, or in the adjoining rooms that could have started a fire. All the lights were off in the room where the fire was first discovered.

### Names of 31 Victims

Here, drawn up by the nuns of the Grey Nuns, is the list of children bearing family names who perished in the fire last Thursday:

Edwin Mansfield, Bernadette Dauphinais, Annette Deschênes, Laurent Mailloux, Rosario Trudeau, Marie-Yvette-Estherine Larochelle, Marie-Madeleine-Alice Larochelle, Émérentienne Cadieux, Jos.-Napoléon Moisan, Joseph Benoit, Madeleine-Ysette Duval, Philippe Vandal, Joseph-Armand-Henri Drainville, Willie Rochon, Théodorat Kabe, Georges Lizotte, Roland Alarie, Edmond Boucher, Mary-Priscilla Hassan, Stack Steller, Valida Champagne, Mary More, Malcolm Roberts, Lionel Joseph Loiseau, Lucien Horan, Jérémie Roger, Marie Zoturrex, Patricia Covey, Joseph Nichol, Annette Deschênes.

In addition to the catastrophe of some fifty other small children. In the course of the morning, the nuns identified 24 of the 53 bodies that rest at the morgue. They also drew up a list of all the dead. Forty-one bodies were removed from the rubble the night of the fire, and 13 the next day.

Jules Noël, 15 months; Marie-Esthérie Larochelle, 2 years and 8 months; Napoléon Moisan, 6 months; Marie Zoturrex, 2 years and 8 months; Elsie Alberteen, 12 months; Joseph Benoit, 1 year and 10 months; Marie Victorine, 11 days; Alice Larochelle, 9 months; Mary Priscilla Hassan, 8 months; Armand Drainville, 20 months; Jérémie Horan, 3 years; Lionel Loiseau, 20 months; Émérentienne Cadieux, 20 months; Philippe Vandal, 15 months; Patricia Covey, 15 months; Joseph Alarie, 2 years and 1 month; Willie Rochon, 1 year and some months; Madeleine Yvette Duval, 20 months; Joseph Nichol, 16 months; Marguerite (No. 457), 6 months; Joseph Yvonneau, 14 months; Alice Cochrane, 22 months; Annette Deschênes.

### Sub-Chief Marin's Testimony

Sub-Chief of firefighters Marin was the first witness heard at the inquest. He recounted that he was called — the fire having, according to him, broken out in the children's dormitory, next to the Crèche. The next day of the disaster, while searching through the ruins, he discovered the remains of a gas stove. He cannot say where this stove was located at the time of the fire, nor has the cause of the fire been revealed.

"Were you the first to arrive at the scene of the fire?"

"In arriving at the fire, did you discover anything that would lead you to believe the fire had been maliciously set?"

"Nothing at all."

"Did anything suggest the accidental cause of the fire to you?"

"No."

### The Nuns' Testimony

The coroner then questioned the Reverend Mother du Sacré-Cœur, assistant general of the Grey Nuns' community.

She was alerted to the presence of fire in the children's dormitory by the hospital nun who was on the premises and who telephoned her using the community's private telephone. She immediately gave the order to sound the alarm and headed toward the crèche.

She arrived on the top floor almost at the same time as the firefighters; the fire, which was already spreading through the corridor, made access to the dormitory impossible. She could not see where the fire originated.

The next witness was Sister Cordelia. She was the one who supervised the children during the day. Her shift ended around eight o'clock, when the fire broke out, and she was with her replacement in the washroom adjoining the recreation room, which is next to the children's dormitory.

It was from there that she noticed the fire rising from the floor, near a heater, in the dormitory. There was in this room, nor in the others, she said, nothing that could have started a fire — no stove, no heater. The lights had been out in the dormitory for half an hour.

At seven hours and twenty minutes, Sister Cordelia made the rounds of the dormitory.

### The Grey Nuns Received Requests from All Over

The Grey Nuns received from all parts of the province a great number of requests for information about the children who perished in the fire last Thursday.

The nuns, indeed, kept in the convent — in addition to a good number of babies whose mothers, widowed and forced to work, were unable to care for them — orphaned and foundling children.

It would be difficult to say the number of these children who perished, for until now only one body has been identified [by family].

The inquest into the death of the small children took place this morning. No measures have yet been taken regarding the funerals, but we are told at the Grey Nuns' convent that, according to custom, the nuns will bury the small bodies in the plot they own at the Côte-des-Neiges cemetery. The funerals will be without ceremony.

The child who was identified was Lionel Loiseau. His mother had entrusted him to the Grey Nuns. She had attached around his neck, on a small gold chain, half of a locket, of which she had kept the other half. It was thus that she could recognize her child. She wanted to take her child's body away, but the keeper of the morgue replied that she could not do so until after the inquest and with special permission from the municipal authorities.

Hundreds of people, some driven by personal motives, but the majority by charity, visited the morgue yesterday; the spectacle was indeed one to bring tears, and several women, at the sight of the small charred bodies, broke down in tears.

All the sick who on the night of the fire had been transported to various hospitals in the city are doing well today. In a few days they will be able to return to the only home they have ever known: the one provided to them by the charity of the nuns.
