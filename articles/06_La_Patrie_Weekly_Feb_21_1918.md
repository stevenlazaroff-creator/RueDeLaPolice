# Hecatomb at the Grey Nuns' Convent

**Source:** *La Patrie* (Weekly Edition / *Le Cultivateur*), Montreal, Thursday, February 21, 1918

---

## Nuns and Nurses Rush into the Burning Dormitory Where Approximately 170 Babies, None Older Than Two Years, Were Sleeping, and Make Extraordinary Efforts to Snatch the Poor Little Beings from the Devouring Flames

### The Invalid Soldiers Are Rescued at the Same Time by Teams of Comrades and Transported to Nearby Premises

### Petrifying Scenes Unfold as the Flames Reach the Children's Cradles, Whose Cries of Pain Redouble the Energy of the Rescuers

A rumor, coming from the west of the city and which swelled with incredible speed — a terrible and disconcerting rumor because of the sinister consequences it foreshadowed — spread Thursday evening, around eight o'clock, throughout the entire city, bringing news of a catastrophe that no one dared to measure, and casting into every mind the consternation and the grim sadness inspired by a misfortune one feels powerless to avert.

Carried by thousands of mouths, transmitted by chance encounters on the street or on the tramway, commented upon by those who already knew it, the news, winged and diligent in its course, made the round of the city and soon became the pivot of all conversations.

People repeated mechanically: "Fire at the Grey Nuns..." They wanted at first to doubt it and refused to believe, for a moment, in such a terrible misfortune, but soon, rising above the line of high rooftops and filling with its wan light the fog-laden sky, a reddish glow, shifting, above which twisted clouds of black smoke, suddenly confirmed the sad reality.

An immense crowd, driven by curiosity, headed toward Guy Street, from where this center of intense light radiated, and there it remained, until a very late hour of the night, watching the destruction of a part of the mother house of the Reverend Grey Nuns.

Misfortunes, like happiness, are relative, and there are some we accept almost light-heartedly, as inevitable distractions of fate, but the one on Thursday will leave in memory a sorrow whose sadness can never be diminished. For this time, the fire was not content to devour mere matter; it did not simply twist in its blazing embrace a mighty structure or pulverize ancient stones: it attacked human life itself, and it left behind — convulsed by the unbearable pain of its bite — more than fifty children's bodies.

How to express, indeed, the immeasurable sadness of this hecatomb of children who, in the dead of night, while the sleep of innocence gave them, in the whiteness of their cradles, the appearance of little angels, were brutally torn from life and sacrificed in the most atrocious pain one can imagine! How to express above all the feeling inspired by the spectacle of these small half-charred bodies, stiffened in a last and powerless gesture of defense against the fire that enveloped them with its blazing breath.

The fire struck precisely the part of the building devoted to the care of the elderly, the infirm, and wounded soldiers. It brutally cast into the street nearly centenarian old people who had been living in the calm and repose of the institution for many years. It jostled in the same hasty and disorderly retreat before danger, invalid soldiers who could not walk, aged nuns, defenseless children, and nurses whose devotion exposed them several times to death. It was, indeed, with a daring worthy of all praise that the institution's staff applied themselves to the rescue of the children. The speed with which the fire spread did not, however, allow them to complete their task, and this morning, some sixty small bodies were found in the half-burned cradles. Most, recount the rescuers, had their faces buried in the pillow — a last defensive gesture to escape suffocation and the bite of the fire.

*The article was accompanied by two photographs:*

2. *A photograph captioned: "True soldiers that they are, after the harsh experience gathered on the battlefield, the convalescents of the Grey Nuns' military hospital did not delay, from Friday on, in putting their dormitory back in order.
