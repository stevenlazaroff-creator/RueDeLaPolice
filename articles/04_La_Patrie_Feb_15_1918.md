# The Fire at the Grey Nuns' Convent — The Death Toll by 2 P.M. Reaches 53

**Source:** *La Patrie*, Montreal, Friday, February 15, 1918, 39th Year, No. 296 (Front Page)

*Note: This article was transcribed from the scanned front-page image, as no machine-readable text was available for this issue.*

---

## The Appalling Hecatomb Last Night at the Mother House of the Grey Nuns Has Taken On an Unprecedented Character of Horror

Nuns and nurses rushed into the burning dormitory where approximately 170 babies, none older than two years, were sleeping, and made extraordinary efforts to snatch the poor little beings from the devouring flames.

The invalid soldiers were rescued at the same time by teams of comrades and transported to nearby premises.

Petrifying scenes unfolded at the moment when the flames reached the children's cradles, whose cries of pain redoubled the energy of the rescuers.

### Mgr. the Archbishop and the Mayor of Montreal on the Scene

### Inquest Tomorrow Morning

A rumor, coming from the west of the city and which swelled with incredible speed — a terrible and disconcerting rumor because of the sinister consequences it foreshadowed — spread last Thursday evening, around eight o'clock, throughout the entire city, bringing news of a catastrophe that no one dared to measure, and casting into every mind the consternation and the grim sadness inspired by a misfortune one feels powerless to avert.

Carried by thousands of mouths, transmitted by chance encounters on the street or on the tramway, commented upon by those who already knew it, the news, winged and diligent in its course, made the round of the city and soon became the pivot of all conversations.

People repeated mechanically: "Fire at the Grey Nuns..." They wanted at first to doubt it and refused to believe, for a moment, in such a terrible misfortune, but soon, rising above the line of high rooftops and filling with its wan light the fog-laden sky, a reddish glow, shifting, above which twisted clouds of black smoke, suddenly confirmed the sad reality.

### The Cause of the Fire

The fire broke out between the fifth and sixth floors of the institution, and was most likely caused by a short circuit, according to the nuns' account, this morning. On the fifth floor, were the military hospital rooms and on the sixth, the dormitories of the Crèche. Everywhere the fire quickly spread, with it seeping through the floor and with the clouds of smoke that quickly caused asphyxiation among the little ones of the Sainte-Famille room.

The top floor was entirely occupied by children of very young age: babies just a few days old, poor orphans abandoned on the doorstep of the institution and gathered by the charity of the good nuns. The others, who were in other parts of the building, were rescued. Immediately the soldiers — convalescents and others, the wounded — returned from the front, assisted in a remarkable fashion. This part of the building was under the jurisdiction of the Military Hospitals Commission, immediately after the beginning of the war.

### Hundreds of Sick Persons

Some two hundred soldiers were sleeping in the building and others came during the day for treatment. The firefighters arrived under the leadership of Sub-Chief Marin, who was the first on the scene, and he with the soldiers organized the first rescue. The losses were considerable in terms of human life. The firefighters arriving at the Grey Nuns' institution under the direction of Sub-Chief Marin discovered that the dormitory was nearly completely engulfed; the babies' cradles filled with little ones were already being consumed. Nearly everything at that point was ablaze.

The firefighters pushed toward the Crèche at the left of the building. Sub-Chief Marin led the rescuers at once, but a blast of smoke and flame that came from the interior soon made it impossible to reach any further.

### The Mayor Martin Injured

His Honor the Mayor Martin, who was on his way to the scene on Guy Street, was struck by a sleigh and was injured. He was taken to Notre-Dame Hospital.

### The Fire Under Control

At the moment of the calamity, last night, it was impossible to establish the number of those who had perished. It appears that not less than sixty babies were among the dead. It seems that after the rescue was concluded, nearly everything had been completed. The entire part of the building affected by fire is in ruins, and the other parts of the building — the dormitory.

### Visit to the Institution This Morning

Early this morning, reporters from *La Patrie* visited the Grey Nuns' convent. The fire was effectively out. It had been fought, one was told, until about midnight. The only thing that remained of this floor was nothing but ruins and the charred bodies of children.
